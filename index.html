KASHI

A generative Islamic geometry studio

The inspiration

Multan is known for kashi, its blue and white glazed tilework, which covers shrines such as the tomb of Shah Rukhn-e-Alam. Craftsmen built these star patterns with a compass and a straightedge, following rules handed down through generations. Kashi asks a simple question: can those rules be written as code?

The method

Kashi uses the polygons-in-contact method, described by E. H. Hankin in the 1920s and later formalised by computer scientist Craig S. Kaplan.

  1. Cover the plane with a simple tiling of polygons.
  2. From the midpoint of every edge, send two rays inward at the contact angle θ.
  3. Stop each ray where it meets a ray from the neighbouring edge.

The meeting points form the stars. Changing a single number, θ, turns the same skeleton into a completely different pattern. Press T to see the skeleton underneath.
